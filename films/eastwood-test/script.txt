In the 1950s, Universal Pictures fired a young contract actor. Burt Reynolds, who was let go at the same time, remembered the reasons. He talked too slow. He had a chipped tooth he wouldn't fix. And his Adam's apple stuck out too far. That actor was Clint Eastwood.

# 1964 | Fifteen Thousand Dollars
A few years later, he was playing the second lead on a TV Western called Rawhide. Then a little-known Italian director named Sergio Leone offered him a Western, shot in Spain. Henry Fonda, Charles Bronson and James Coburn had already said no. The pay was fifteen thousand dollars.

> Clint Eastwood: I was doing Rawhide, and I was coming to a hiatus

He took it anyway. The film was A Fistful of Dollars. It cost about two hundred thousand dollars to make, and it turned a TV cowboy into the most famous face in Westerns. Burt Reynolds told him he was the one in real trouble that day. A man can learn to act. Nobody gets rid of an Adam's apple.
